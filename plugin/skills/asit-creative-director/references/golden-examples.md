# Golden Examples — ASIT creative references v0.3.8

Not runnable lesson modules or source-proven images. These are concise authoring standards; always choose source-valid assets and truthful native controls.

## Strong: "The map changed" (visual positions)

A real symbolic floorplan displays a bike, desk, box and door. Learner predicts where a lost item is, presses a native control to view a second **clearly labeled state**, then writes a short location message to a new recipient. The semantic diagram genuinely supplies the observed relation; text alone cannot solve the perception step. The last message uses a different place/object; no answer leak.

## Weak: "Move day" decorative questionnaire

Five repeated is/are radios and an unrelated apartment stock image labelled "interactive adventure". Nothing changes when a learner makes a decision. Reject; redesign around a meaningful visual observation or honest focused drill without decorative claims.

## Strong: "Customer support triage" (professional English)

A realistic customer message and two internal notes create a conflict. Learner selects the most important evidence, repairs a vague response, and writes a respectful fresh reply to another customer. Compact editorial document view, no pointless photo or SVG; interactivity is in evidence selection and content creation.

## Weak: fake "listening game"

An external audio link of unknown length, generic questions derived only from the title and a Play button that does not actually play. Reject as scored listening; verify a specific short segment and the spoken content, or switch to a clearly labeled reading mode.

## Strong: "Conversation consequence"

A landlord asks about a practical issue. Learner chooses a contextually appropriate action; native UI visibly updates the next *written* landlord message. Free text for a **new scenario** gets semantic teacher feedback after Submit. It is a simulated decision chain, not a claim of genuine live multi-turn voice interaction.

## Anti-repetition comparisons

A new lesson must change at least one **substantive** design dimension from the last two verified lessons: scenario stakes, evidence medium, main cognitive operation, or communication outcome. Replacing only a character name, icon, object or palette is not novelty. Don't invent a lesson-history comparison when history has not been read.
