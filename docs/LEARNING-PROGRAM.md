# ASIT — adaptive learning program

A personal program is a **revisable competency graph**, never an inflexible textbook calendar. ASIT must not assume first language, biography, current level, occupation, schedule or desired target. Those come from the learner or observed performance.

## 1. Start from communicative outcomes

Ask what the learner needs to **do** in English, with whom and in which context. Translate that goal into 3–7 small, assessable can-do competencies, each with a stable private ID, prerequisite IDs, priority, scope (modality and operation), CEFR reference when defensible, rubric and relevant contexts. Illustrative goals such as travel, reading fiction, or workplace communication are examples, not installed defaults.

## 2. Establish a provisional baseline

Self-ratings are hypotheses, not measurements. Sample independent meaning-making, comprehension and interaction. Observe task achievement, relevant grammar and vocabulary, help needed, and use in a changed context. Mark anything not observed `unassessed`, especially listening/pronunciation when real audio is unavailable. Never claim a global CEFR level from one answer.

## 3. Keep a rolling path

Show a **current** can-do goal, **next** two or three dependencies or adjacent goals, small **due review** set, and **later** goals. Do not fabricate a 90-day schedule before collecting learning evidence. Reprioritize when the learner explicitly changes goals; keep previous attempts, explain the revision, and use state_revision so cached plans can be rejected.

## 4. Teach through evidence

Choose a focused communicative situation, retrieve a useful pattern, offer minimal model/scaffolding, practise guided production, then test independent meaningful use and transfer. Short sessions compress stages; free chat preserves flow. Activity formats change because the skill being tested changes, not to produce random widgets.

Assess attempts with separate dimensions: task meaning, independence/hint level, intelligibility/accuracy, interaction and transfer. An option choice can show recognition, but not oral production. Typed English cannot establish pronunciation, speaking latency or genuine audio comprehension. Require suitable independent contexts and sessions before marking transfer, and allow later contrary evidence to reduce the current status without erasing history.

## 5. Revisit and refine

Schedule a compact retrieval queue linked to real weak or useful patterns. The current review intervals are a simple **heuristic**, not FSRS or a calibrated forgetting model. After several sessions review progression, target usefulness, recurring errors and ability to transfer in new situations. Re-diagnose if observed performance changes sharply. No invented mastery percentage or predicted date to reach B2.

## Quality bar

A lesson is suitable only when an informed teacher could identify: the learner goal, the target can-do skill, the rubric, why each stage exists, the learner's independently produced evidence, a helpful correction and an appropriate next step.

Pedagogical reference: Council of Europe, CEFR Companion Volume (2020), communicative can-do descriptors. Principles include formative assessment, task-based communication, retrieval and later meaningful reuse. This codebase does not claim measured educational efficacy.
