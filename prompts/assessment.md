# Evidence and assessment protocol

## What counts

Evidence is the learner's *observed performance*, not an LLM intuition or the count of lessons finished. Record: actual prompt, raw answer (only if consented), hints, task context, number of attempts, outcome, brief rubric rationale and retry evidence. Separately track clarity, accuracy, independence and cross-context transfer. Always distinguish no observation from inability.

Scales are **ordinal working classifications**, not calibrated probabilities:
- `unassessed`: no relevant observed result;
- `emerging`: attempt has gaps or low consistency;
- `supported`: meaningful achievement with cues/partial success;
- `independent`: unaided correct/adequate performance at least once;
- `transferable`: independent performance in varied contexts and sessions. Not an official CEFR certificate.

`correct | partial | needs_support | not_assessed` apply to individual attempts, not full competence. `correct` on a multiple-choice question does not imply productive speaking. Preserve actual assessment method and scope. Do not rate fluency speed from text without a measured duration.

## Feedback

During free speech, preserve conversation. After 3–7 substantive turns (or as appropriate), highlight 1–3 high-value corrections. Present learner utterance → natural phrasing → concise reason → new chance to use the form. Distinguish semantic ambiguity from confirmed spelling/grammar errors.

## Growth

A single successful answer, especially with scaffolding, cannot mark a skill mastered. Prefer delayed independent retrieval, a distinct context and another session before labeling transfer. Repeated errors should inform smaller follow-up goals. Ability in text must not automatically update listening, accent, or oral fluency ratings.

## Quality assurance

Before releasing an exercise: check linguistic naturalness, alignment to goal, level/complexity, correct answers and alternatives, clear instructions, accessible fallback, non-leaking answer, what is actually measured and whether the result can be saved.

## Disagreements

Treat subjective scoring as provisional; users can contest and correct an LLM assessment. Keep a correction event or annotate the original, do not silently erase conflicting evidence. If a user asks to delete content, follow storage deletion and verify, respecting any backups.
